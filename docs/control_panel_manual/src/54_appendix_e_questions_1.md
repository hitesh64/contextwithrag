# Appendix E. Questions and Answers for Operators and Technicians

The questions below are the ones most often asked on the shop floor and in training. Each answer points to the chapter with the details. They can also be used as quiz questions during skill assessment (Chapter 30).

## E.1 Safety and LOTO

**Q1. When is LOTO required?**
For every job that puts any part of your body into a danger zone of a machine or brings you close to parts that could be live: clearing a jam inside a guard, changing tools, adjusting, cleaning inside, repairing and inspecting (Chapter 3.4).

**Q2. What are the eight steps of LOTO?**
Prepare, inform, shut down, isolate, lock and tag, release stored energy, verify zero energy, then do the work and remove LOTO safely in reverse order (Chapter 3.4.2).

**Q3. What does "test - test - test" mean?**
Before touching a conductor, test the voltage tester on a known live source, test the circuit (phase to phase and phase to earth), then test the tester again on the live source. Only then is the circuit proved dead.

**Q4. Can I remove another person's lock if they have gone home?**
No. Only the maintenance manager may remove it, after trying to contact the owner, checking the equipment personally and recording the removal in the LOTO register. The owner must be told before starting work the next day (Chapter 3.4.3).

**Q5. How is LOTO handed over at shift change?**
The incoming person puts on his or her own lock first, then the outgoing person removes theirs, so the isolation point is never without a lock (Chapter 3.4.4).

**Q6. Which energy sources must be isolated on BRAKE-04?**
Electrical (main isolator), hydraulic (pump stopped, pressure released, gauge at zero) and gravity (ram lowered onto blocks or held by the ram support) (Chapter 9.5.2).

**Q7. Which extinguisher is used on a fire in a live electrical panel?**
CO2 or clean agent. Never water or foam on live equipment (Chapter 3.9).

**Q8. What PPE is required at the pre-treatment tanks?**
Chemical goggles, PVC or nitrile gauntlets, PVC apron and gumboots, for any work at the tanks including taking samples (Chapter 12.5).

**Q9. What should I do if a safety device fails its daily test?**
Report it at once. The machine must not be used until maintenance has repaired and re-tested the device and the supervisor has signed the check sheet (Chapter 3.6).

**Q10. What is the first action for someone who received an electric shock?**
Do not touch the person until the supply is switched off, or move them away using dry wood. Check breathing, start CPR if needed and call the first aider and doctor (Chapter 3.10).

**Q11. What should you do after high-pressure hydraulic oil hits your skin?**
Treat it as a medical emergency even if it looks small: go to hospital immediately and say "high pressure injection injury" (Chapter 26.4).

**Q12. Why is orange wiring dangerous?**
Orange wires are interlock circuits fed from an external source; they stay live even when the panel's own incomer is off. Isolate the external source before working on them (Chapter 17.3).

## E.2 Sheet metal machines

**Q13. What blade gap is used on SHEAR-01 for 2 mm CRCA?**
0.10 mm, with a rake angle of 1.0 degree. The CNC sets it from the material and thickness in the program (Chapter 6.7.1).

**Q14. What is the maximum thickness SHEAR-01 can cut?**
6 mm mild steel (450 N/mm2), 4 mm stainless steel, 8 mm aluminium, over 3100 mm length (Chapter 6.3).

**Q15. Why must strips narrower than 15 mm not be cut on SHEAR-01?**
Narrow strips twist and bow. Strips narrower than 15 mm are cut on LASER-03 (Chapter 6.7.4).

**Q16. What does alarm SH-12 mean?**
Hold-down pressure not reached: low pressure or a hold-down leak. Check the pressure switch and hold-down seals (Chapter 6.11.3; case study 28.3).

**Q17. What punch-die clearance is used on PUNCH-02 for stainless steel 304?**
25 to 30 % of the thickness (total clearance). For 2.0 mm, 0.50 to 0.60 mm (Chapter 7.7.1).

**Q18. How is the punching force calculated?**
Force (kN) = cut perimeter (mm) x thickness (mm) x shear strength (N/mm2) / 1000. Use 350 N/mm2 for mild steel, 550 for stainless 304 and 200 for aluminium 5052 (Chapter 7.7.2).

**Q19. What is the maximum force that may be programmed on PUNCH-02?**
80 % of the machine force, that is 24 tonnes (Chapter 7.7.2).

**Q20. When must a turret punch be sharpened?**
When the cutting edge radius reaches 0.25 mm, or when burrs exceed 10 % of the thickness. Remove only 0.1 to 0.2 mm per sharpening (Chapter 7.10.5).

**Q21. What does alarm TP-06 mean?**
Clamp dead zone violation: a hit is programmed inside the clamp zone. Edit the program or use repositioning (Chapter 7.11.1).

**Q22. Which gas is used to cut stainless steel on LASER-03?**
Nitrogen (99.99 % purity), at high pressure (14 to 16 bar for 1.5 to 3 mm). Oxygen is used for thick mild steel (Chapter 8.7.1).

**Q23. How often is the laser protective window checked?**
Every shift at start-up, and whenever the cut quality drops. Clean with lens tissue and isopropyl alcohol; replace if cracked, burnt or pitted (Chapter 8.7.2).

**Q24. What does sparks flying upward during laser cutting mean?**
The cut is not through: speed too high, power too low, focus wrong or window dirty. Stop before the head gets spatter (Chapter 8.7.5).

**Q25. What are the chiller set points of LASER-03?**
Laser circuit 25 degrees C, optics circuit 28 degrees C. The laser must not be started before the chiller is ready (Chapter 8.3, 8.6).

**Q26. What does alarm LS-09 mean?**
Height sensor calibration failed: dirty nozzle, loose ceramic or poor earthing of the sheet (rusty slats) (Chapter 8.11.1).

**Q27. Why must the laser never cut unattended?**
Laser cutting can start fires in the slag tray, fume duct or filter (Chapter 8.5).

**Q28. Which V-die is used to bend 2 mm mild steel?**
V16 (about 8 x thickness). Force about 16 tonnes per metre, inside radius 2.6 mm, minimum flange 11 mm (Chapter 9.7.1).

**Q29. What is the press brake force formula?**
Force (tonnes per metre) = 64 x thickness2 / V for mild steel of 450 N/mm2. Multiply by 1.5 for stainless 304 and 0.5 for aluminium 5052 (Chapter 9.7.1).

**Q30. Can 6 mm mild steel be bent over 3 m on BRAKE-04 with a V50 die?**
No. It needs 46 x 3 = 138 tonnes, more than the 135 tonne capacity (Chapter 9.7.1).

**Q31. What is the bend deduction for 2 mm CRCA with a V16 die?**
3.8 mm per 90 degree bend. A U-channel 40 + 100 + 40 mm has a flat length of 180 - 2 x 3.8 = 172.4 mm (Chapter 9.7.2).

**Q32. How is the BRAKE-04 laser guard tested every day?**
With a 14 mm test rod on the die at the left end, centre and right end: in fast approach, the ram must stop before touching the rod (Chapter 9.5.1).

**Q33. Angles are good at the ends but open in the middle. What is wrong?**
Crowning is too low. Increase crowning in the CNC (Chapter 9.9 note; case study 28.1).

**Q34. What does alarm PB-12 mean?**
Back gauge X axis fault: drive, encoder or obstruction. Remove the obstruction, reset the drive, check the encoder and its cable (Chapter 9.11.1).

**Q35. Why should short parts be bent in the centre of the press brake?**
Concentrated load at one end damages tools and the beam (Chapter 9.7.1).

## E.3 Welding, hardware and finishing

**Q36. What is the weld schedule for 1.6 + 1.6 mm CRCA on WELD-05?**
Electrode face 6.3 mm, force 3.5 kN, 14 cycles, about 11 kA, minimum nugget 5.1 mm (Chapter 10.2.4).

**Q37. How often are spot welding tips dressed?**
Every 300 welds on mild steel, every 100 on galvanised sheet, or whenever the peel test shows a small nugget (Chapter 10.2.8).

**Q38. How is a peel test accepted?**
A button (nugget) pulls out of one strip, with a diameter at least equal to the minimum nugget diameter (Chapter 10.2.7).

**Q39. Which shielding gas and flow are used for MIG welding?**
82 % argon / 18 % CO2 at 12 to 15 litres per minute (Chapter 10.3).

**Q40. What causes porosity in MIG welds?**
Gas flow too low or too high, draught, dirty surface, nozzle blocked by spatter (Chapter 10.3.7).

**Q41. Why are self-clinching fasteners inserted before painting?**
So that the paint is not cracked by pressing, and threads can be masked before coating (Chapter 11.1).

**Q42. What hole size is used for an M5 self-clinching nut?**
6.35 mm, in sheet of at least 1.0 mm (Chapter 11.3.2).

**Q43. Why must the insertion side of a clinch hole not be deburred or chamfered?**
The sheet metal must flow into the fastener's undercut; a chamfer removes that metal and the fastener pushes out (Chapter 11.2).

**Q44. Why are wire passage holes fitted with grommets?**
A sharp edge can cut through insulation by vibration over months and cause an earth fault (Chapter 11.4.1).

## E.4 Paint shop

**Q45. What are the seven stages of PTL-08?**
Alkaline degreasing, water rinse, acid pickling, water rinse, activation, zinc phosphating, DM water rinse with passivation, followed by the dry-off oven (Chapter 12.2).

**Q46. What is the target phosphate coating weight?**
1.5 to 2.5 g per m2 for powder coating (Chapter 12.8.1).

**Q47. What is the water-break test?**
After the rinse following degreasing, water should form a continuous film for 30 seconds. Breaks or beads show oil left on the surface (Chapter 12.8.2).

**Q48. What is the free acid range of the phosphate bath?**
0.8 to 1.2 points; total acid 18 to 22 points (Chapter 12.8).

**Q49. What is the cure schedule of standard polyester powder?**
180 degrees C metal temperature for 10 minutes (or 200 degrees C for 8 minutes). The oven air is set at 200 degrees C (Chapter 13.2.3).

**Q50. What dry film thickness is required on indoor panels?**
60 to 80 microns; no single reading below 50 microns (Chapter 13.8).

**Q51. Why do inside corners stay thinly coated?**
The Faraday cage effect: field lines go to the outer edges. Coat corners first with the manual gun at lower kV (Chapter 13.7.1).

**Q52. Why must the powder booth fan never be switched off while spraying?**
Powder in air can explode; the airflow keeps the concentration below the explosive limit (Chapter 13.5).

**Q53. What earth resistance must the part hooks have?**
Below 1 megohm from part to earth (Chapter 13.5).

**Q54. Why are base frames run in a separate batch?**
Thick parts heat slowly and are under-cured at normal conveyor speed; they run at 1.0 m/min (Chapter 13.7.4; case study 28.8).

**Q55. What does alarm PC-02 mean?**
Flame (or spark) detected in the booth: guns and fan stop automatically. Check the booth, follow the fire procedure and find the cause before restart (Chapter 13.11; case study 28.13).
