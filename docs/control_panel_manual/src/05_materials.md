## 5.2 Receiving inspection

The store and quality department check every incoming lot before it is stocked.

| Check | How | Accept if |
|---|---|---|
| Thickness | Micrometer at 5 points across the sheet (centre and four corners, 25 mm in from the edge) | Within the tolerance of the standard, e.g. 2.0 mm sheet: 1.90 to 2.10 mm |
| Size | Measuring tape, diagonals | Length and width within +5 / -0 mm; diagonals within 3 mm |
| Flatness | Sheet laid on the flat table, feeler gauge | Gap below 3 mm per metre |
| Surface | Visual under good light | No rust, scale, deep scratches, oil stains or pits |
| Coating (GI) | Coating thickness gauge | Average 17 microns or more for 120 g/m2 |
| Busbar dimensions | Vernier, tape | Thickness +/- 0.1 mm, width +/- 0.3 mm |
| Busbar surface | Visual | Bright, no oxide patches, no edge cracks |
| Certificates | Mill test certificate | Grade, heat number and chemical analysis match the order |

A lot that fails is kept in the red quarantine area with a "HOLD" tag until the quality manager decides.

## 5.3 Storage rules

- Store sheets flat on racks or pallets, never leaning against a wall, which bends them and makes handling dangerous.
- Separate the stacks by thickness and grade, and label each rack position with the thickness, grade and size.
- Keep CRCA sheets dry: rust starts within days in a humid monsoon atmosphere. Keep the original oil film; do not wipe it off until the sheet is cut.
- Stainless steel must be kept away from carbon steel. Carbon steel dust embedded in stainless steel causes rust spots. Use separate racks, separate trolleys and, where possible, a separate protective film.
- Store copper busbars horizontally on wooden or plastic supports. Copper must not touch the floor, cement, acids or chlorides.
- Follow first-in, first-out (FIFO). The oldest lot is issued first.

## 5.4 Sheet handling

!! WARNING: Sheet edges are as sharp as a knife. Always wear cut-resistant gloves and full sleeves when handling sheets, blanks and cut parts. Never slide your hand along a sheet edge.

### 5.4.1 Manual handling

| Sheet size | Thickness | Approximate weight | Handling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1250 x 2500 mm | 1.6 mm | 39 kg | Two persons |
| 1250 x 2500 mm | 2.0 mm | 49 kg | Two persons or vacuum lifter |
| 1250 x 2500 mm | 3.0 mm | 74 kg | Vacuum lifter only |
| 1500 x 3000 mm | 2.0 mm | 71 kg | Vacuum lifter only |
| 1500 x 3000 mm | 3.0 mm | 106 kg | Vacuum lifter only |

The weight of a steel sheet is calculated as length (m) x width (m) x thickness (mm) x 7.85 kg. For example, 2.5 m x 1.25 m x 2.0 mm x 7.85 = 49 kg. For aluminium use 2.7 instead of 7.85, for copper 8.9.

### 5.4.2 Vacuum sheet lifter

The vacuum lifter has suction pads, a vacuum pump, a vacuum reservoir, a vacuum gauge and an alarm. It hangs from the jib crane in Bay 1.

1. Check that the pads are clean and not cut, and that the vacuum gauge needle is in the green zone with the pads closed against a sheet.
2. Place the lifter in the centre of the sheet so that the load is balanced.
3. Switch on the vacuum and wait until the gauge is in the green zone (at least -0.6 bar) before lifting.
4. Lift slowly; keep the sheet low, not more than 300 mm above the floor or table.
5. Never stand under the sheet, never lift over people.
6. If the low-vacuum alarm sounds, lower the sheet immediately to the nearest support.

## 5.5 Nesting and material utilisation

Nesting is the arrangement of many parts on one sheet so that the least material is wasted. The programmer prepares a nest for LASER-03 or PUNCH-02 with the CAD/CAM software. The nest report shows each part, the quantity, the sheet size and the material utilisation.

- A good nest uses more than 80 % of the sheet. Below 70 %, the programmer must try to add small common parts (brackets, covers, cable clamps) of other jobs with the same material and thickness.
- Remnants larger than 300 x 300 mm are marked with thickness and grade, and returned to the remnant rack for small parts.
- The nest report is printed and given to the operator together with the job card.

## 5.6 Scrap

| Scrap type | Bin colour | Disposal |
|---|---|---|
| Mild steel offcuts and skeletons | Grey | Sold to scrap dealer by weight |
| Stainless steel scrap | Blue | Kept separate, sold at a higher price |
| Copper and aluminium scrap | Yellow (locked) | Weighed and recorded; sold under supervision |
| Punching slugs | Grey small bin | With MS scrap |
| Powder coating waste powder | Black, closed | Disposed through the authorised waste agency |
| Chemical sludge | Drums, labelled | Hazardous waste, authorised agency only |

!! NOTE: Copper scrap is valuable. Every busbar offcut longer than 300 mm goes back to the busbar rack with its size marked; shorter pieces go into the locked yellow bin. The busbar shop records copper issued and copper returned for every job.
