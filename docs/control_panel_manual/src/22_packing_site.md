# 22. Packing, Dispatch and Site Installation

## 22.1 Preparing the panel for dispatch

1. Receive the dispatch clearance from quality (test report signed, FAT release if applicable).
2. Separate the shipping sections: remove the inter-section bolts and the main busbar links between sections; mark each link and its bolts with the section numbers; pack the links and bolts in a labelled bag fixed inside the receiving section.
3. Secure all moving parts: draw-out ACBs are racked to the connected position and fixed with transport brackets, or removed and packed separately if the maker requires; withdrawable modules are locked in.
4. Fix loose items: door keys (in a bag tied to the incomer door handle), racking handles, ACB charging handles, spare fuses, manuals.
5. Close and lock all doors; tape the door edges if the panel will travel on an open truck.
6. Clean the outside, check the paint, touch up small damage.

## 22.2 Packing

| Transport | Packing |
|---|---|
| Local, closed truck, short distance | Polyethylene (LDPE) wrap with silica gel bags inside each section, wooden skid under base frame, corner protectors |
| Long distance by road | As above plus wooden crate or wooden frame with plywood sides, base skid bolted to the base frame, "this side up", "fragile", centre of gravity and lifting marks |
| Sea freight (export) | Seaworthy wooden case, fumigated wood (ISPM 15 marked), vacuum or VCI (vapour corrosion inhibitor) barrier bag with desiccant, humidity indicator card, shock and tilt indicators |

- Silica gel: about 1 kg per cubic metre of enclosed volume for domestic transport.
- Mark each package with: customer, project, job number, panel name, section number "1 of 4", gross weight, dimensions, lifting points, centre of gravity.
- Fix the packing list in a waterproof pouch on the outside of package 1, and a copy inside.

## 22.3 Loading

!! WARNING: Panels are tall and narrow, with a high centre of gravity. They tip over easily when lifted from one side or when the truck brakes. Lift only at the marked lifting points with a spreader beam, keep the panel upright, and lash it to the truck bed with at least two straps in each direction over wooden blocks (never over the bare door).

1. Check the truck: flat clean bed, no protruding nails, cover (tarpaulin) available.
2. Lift with CRANE-16 (or forklift under the skid with forks spread fully) as per section 3.7.
3. Place the panel with its back towards the truck cabin (the doors face backwards), on rubber or wooden packing pieces.
4. Lash and block. Check that nothing can move.
5. Cover with tarpaulin.
6. Hand over the delivery documents (packing list, delivery challan, invoice, e-way bill as applicable) to the driver and record the vehicle number.

## 22.4 Instructions to the customer for receiving and storage

These instructions are printed on a label fixed to every package and included in the installation manual.

- Inspect on arrival for transport damage before unloading; photograph any damage and record it on the delivery document.
- Unload with a crane or forklift of adequate capacity using the marked lifting points; never roll the panel on pipes without the skid.
- Store indoors in a clean, dry place; if outdoor storage cannot be avoided, keep on a raised platform under a cover, and keep the space heaters energised if possible.
- Do not remove the packing until installation.
- Store for no longer than 3 months before installation without re-inspection; long storage requires IR test before energising.

## 22.5 Installation guidance (for site teams)

### 22.5.1 Foundation and positioning

- The panel stands on a level foundation or a base channel (ISMC) grouted in the floor, level within 1 mm per metre. A trench or cable cellar under the panel is provided as per the GA.
- Keep the front clearance required for operation and maintenance: at least 1000 mm in front of the panel (1500 mm for draw-out ACB panels so that the breaker can be withdrawn), and at least 600 mm behind rear-access panels, or as required by local regulations.
- Place the sections in order, align them, and bolt them together with the inter-section bolts.
- Fix the base frame to the base channel with bolts or by welding at the points shown.

### 22.5.2 Busbar and earth connection between sections

1. Clean the busbar link faces (Chapter 16.4.4).
2. Fit the links with the bolts from the marked bag; torque to Table 16-3; mark with paint.
3. Connect the PE bar links between sections.
4. Connect the main earth terminals of the panel to the site earth grid at two separate points with the earth conductor sized as per the site design.

### 22.5.3 Cables

- Use cable glands of the correct size and type (double compression brass glands for armoured cables); earth the armour through the gland and an earth tag.
- Single-core cables: use non-magnetic (aluminium) gland plates, so that eddy currents do not heat the plate.
- Dress cores neatly to the terminals; support heavy cables on cable supports so that the weight does not hang on the terminals.
- Crimp lugs with the correct die; torque to the device maker's values.
- Seal unused gland holes.

### 22.5.4 Pre-commissioning checks

| Check | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Visual | No transport damage; all covers in place; no foreign material inside |
| Torque | Inter-section links and cable terminations torqued and marked |
| IR test of busbars and cables | Panel busbars with incoming cable: 1000 V DC, above 100 megohm (cables as per their own standard) |
| Earth connections | Two earth connections to grid; continuity |
| Settings | Breaker releases and relays set as per the approved protection setting chart |
| Control supply | Correct voltage before closing any breaker |
| Phase sequence | Checked at the incomer after energising, before starting motors |
| Interlocks | Operated once more after installation |
| Heaters | Energised to dry the panel in humid weather before first energisation |

### 22.5.5 First energisation

1. Inform all persons; remove all earths and tools; close all doors.
2. Keep all outgoing breakers OFF.
3. Close the incomer; check voltages and phase sequence on the meter.
4. Close outgoing feeders one by one; observe for any abnormal sound, smell or reading.
5. After 24 hours of load operation, and again after one month, check busbar joints and cable terminations with a thermal imaging camera; tighten any joint that shows a temperature more than 10 degrees C above similar joints (after isolation and LOTO).

## 22.6 Maintenance advice for the customer

| Interval | Task |
|---|---|
| Monthly | Visual check, cleaning of filters, check heaters and fans, test RCCBs, check indications |
| Every 6 months | Thermal imaging of joints and terminations under load |
| Yearly | Shutdown maintenance: clean inside with vacuum, check torque of busbar joints and terminations (isolate and LOTO), operate all breakers, check ACB as per Chapter 19.4.4, IR test, check interlocks and earth connections |
| After any fault trip | Inspect the breaker and the affected circuit before re-closing |

!! HINGLISH: Dispatch se pehle quality ka clearance aur test report zaroori. Shipping section alag karte waqt busbar link aur bolt par section number likho aur bag mein rakho. Panel sirf lifting point aur spreader beam se uthao, panel jaldi palat jaata hai. Truck mein dono taraf strap se baandho. Site par sections jodkar link ko Table 16-3 ke torque par kaso, do jagah earth lagao, IR test karo, phir incomer on karke phase sequence check karo.
