# 4. Roles, Shift Working and Workplace Discipline

## 4.2 Duties of the machine operator

The operator is the first person to notice when a machine starts to go wrong. A good operator keeps the machine clean, does the daily checks honestly, and reports small problems before they become breakdowns.

At the start of the shift:

1. Read the shift log and the handover notes of the previous shift.
2. Wear the PPE required for the machine.
3. Carry out the daily check sheet of the machine and sign it. Report any failed item to the supervisor before starting.
4. Collect the job card, the drawing and the program number for the first job.

During the shift:

1. Set up the job as per the drawing and program; run the first piece and get it approved (first-off approval).
2. Check parts at the frequency given on the job card and record the results.
3. Keep the work area clean; remove scrap and offcuts into the scrap bins.
4. Report every alarm, unusual noise, smell, vibration or leak to the supervisor and record it in the machine log.

At the end of the shift:

1. Finish or stop the job at a safe point; return the machine to its home position.
2. Clean the machine and the area as per the cleaning instructions in the machine chapter.
3. Fill the production record and the shift log.
4. Hand over to the next operator in person, explaining any open problems.

## 4.3 Duties of the maintenance technician

- Respond to breakdown calls within 10 minutes for critical machines and 30 minutes for others.
- Follow LOTO for every intervention.
- Record each breakdown in the breakdown register with: asset code, date and time of call, start and end of repair, symptom, cause, action taken, parts used, and whether the root cause is known.
- Carry out PM tasks on the scheduled date; record readings (pressures, temperatures, currents, vibrations).
- Raise a spare part request when a part is used, so that the minimum stock is restored.
- Never leave a machine with a bypassed safety device. A temporary repair must be tagged, recorded and followed by a permanent repair.
- Hand over the machine to the operator after repair with a trial run in the presence of the operator.

## 4.4 Shift timings and handover

The plant works in two shifts of eight and a half hours, with a 30-minute lunch break. The paint shop and the compressor may run a third shift when the order book requires it.

| Shift | Time |
|---|---|
| A (general / first) | 08:00 to 16:30 |
| B (second) | 16:30 to 01:00 |
| C (third, as needed) | 01:00 to 08:00 (paint shop only) |

### 4.4.1 Shift handover

Handover is done face to face at the machine, never through a note alone. The outgoing person tells the incoming person:

- which job is running, how many pieces are done and how many remain;
- the program number and the tooling in the machine;
- any alarm or problem in the shift, and whether it is solved;
- any open LOTO, permit or maintenance job;
- any instruction from the supervisor that applies to the next shift.

The shift log of each bay has a handover section signed by both people.

## 4.5 5S in the workplace

5S is a method for keeping a workplace clean, orderly and safe. It is applied in every bay and audited every month.

| S | Japanese word | Meaning in practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Seiri (Sort) | Remove everything not needed for the work: old drawings, broken tools, scrap, unused parts |
| 2 | Seiton (Set in order) | A marked place for everything: tool shadow boards, labelled racks, floor markings |
| 3 | Seiso (Shine) | Clean the machine and area daily; cleaning is also inspection, as it shows leaks and loose parts |
| 4 | Seiketsu (Standardise) | Write the rules: cleaning charts, check sheets, photographs of the correct state |
| 5 | Shitsuke (Sustain) | Follow the rules every day; supervisors audit and correct |

### 4.5.1 5S audit scoring

| Score | Condition |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not started: area dirty, items scattered |
| 1 | Some sorting done, many unneeded items remain |
| 2 | Sorted and set in order, cleaning irregular |
| 3 | Clean and orderly, standards written |
| 4 | Standards followed every day, problems corrected by the team itself |

Each bay is scored on 20 points (four items per S). The target is 16 or more.

## 4.6 Tools and tool control

- Each fitter and wireman has a personal tool kit listed on a tool card. The kit is checked monthly.
- Torque wrenches and crimping tools are calibrated every six months (torque wrenches) and every year (crimping tools) by the quality department. A calibration sticker shows the due date. A tool past its due date must not be used.
- Before a panel is closed, the fitter counts the tools back into the kit. A tool left inside a panel can cause a short circuit when the panel is energised.

!! DANGER: A spanner, screw or wire offcut left on a busbar can cause a flash-over when the panel is switched on. Before closing any compartment, look, count your tools and remove all loose material. The testing engineer repeats this check before the HV test.

## 4.7 Training and skill matrix

Every worker is trained before being allowed to work alone. Training includes the general safety induction, the machine or job training under an experienced operator, and an assessment by the supervisor.

| Skill level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Not trained |
| 1 | Under training; may work only with a trainer |
| 2 | Can work alone on standard jobs |
| 3 | Can set up and work on all jobs, solve common problems |
| 4 | Can train others |

The skill matrix of each bay is displayed on the notice board. Each machine needs at least two operators at level 3 or above in each shift pattern, so that absence does not stop the machine.

## 4.8 Communication on the shop floor

- **Morning meeting (10 minutes, at 08:10)**: safety point of the day, yesterday's output and problems, today's plan.
- **Andon / call system**: each machine has a call button with lamps: green for running, yellow for "material or quality help needed", red for "maintenance needed".
- **Breakdown call**: press the red call button and phone the maintenance desk with the asset code and a short description, for example "BRAKE-04, back gauge not moving, alarm PB-12".
- **Visual boards**: production board (plan versus actual per hour), quality board (rejections), safety board (days without injury, near misses).
